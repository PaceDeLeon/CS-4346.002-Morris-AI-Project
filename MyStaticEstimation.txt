CS 4346 Morris Game, Variant - Improved Static Estimation
=========================================================

Code:     game/improved_evaluation.py
Programs: MiniMaxOpeningImproved.py, MiniMaxGameImproved.py
          (same MINIMAX search, move generator, and input/output as
          MiniMaxOpening.py / MiniMaxGame.py; only the estimate changes)

All scores are from White's point of view: positive favors White.
Non-terminal midgame/endgame scores are kept strictly between -10000 and
10000 so they never look like a win or a loss.


1. Features
-----------

Each feature is computed for both players and used as a difference
(White minus Black), so the function treats both sides the same way.

  pieces      Number of pieces on the board.

  threats     Number of empty spots where the player can close a mill on
              their next move: two of their pieces are on a line, the third
              spot is empty, and they can get a piece there.
                - Opening: any piece can be placed there.
                - Midgame: one of their pieces outside that line must be
                  adjacent to the empty spot.
                - Endgame (exactly 3 pieces): the third piece can hop there.

  mobility    Number of (piece, destination) moves: adjacent empty spots,
              or every empty spot for a player with 3 pieces in the
              midgame/endgame. In the opening, hopping does not apply, so it
              counts adjacent empty spots only (room to move once the
              midgame starts).

  blocked     Number of the player's pieces with no empty neighbor
              (midgame/endgame only; 0 for a player who can hop).

  mills       Number of completed mills owned by the player
              (midgame/endgame only).

  terminal    Midgame/endgame wins and losses:
                Black has 2 or fewer pieces   -> +10000
                White has 2 or fewer pieces   -> -10000
                Black has no legal move       -> +10000
                White has no legal move       -> -10000   (new)


2. Why these features
---------------------

pieces   - Material decides the game: a player reduced to 2 pieces loses.
           It stays the dominant term, as in the handout.

threats  - A mill is the only way to win material, so a position that can
           close a mill next move is close to winning a piece. Two or more
           threats are especially strong because the opponent can block only
           one per move. The handout opening estimate counts pieces only, so
           every placement that does not capture right away gets the same
           score and the tie is broken by board index.

mobility - The second way to win is to leave the opponent with no legal move.
           The handout counts only Black's moves; counting both sides also
           rewards White for keeping its own pieces free. In the opening,
           spots with free neighbors matter because the midgame is about
           moving.

blocked  - A piece with no empty neighbor cannot help form or block a mill.
           Blocked opponent pieces are a step toward a no-move win; our own
           blocked pieces are a weakness.

mills    - A completed mill can be opened and closed again to keep
           capturing, and its pieces cannot be captured. It gets a small
           weight because the gain comes from reopening it, which threats
           already measure.

terminal - The handout midgame estimate checks "no legal move" for Black
           only. If White is completely blocked, the handout scores it as an
           ordinary position instead of a loss. For example, in
           WWWWBBBxBBxxxBxxxxxxxxx White's a0, d0, g0, b1 cannot move; the
           handout gives -2009 and the improved version gives -10000.


3. How they are combined
------------------------

A weighted sum of the White-minus-Black differences.

Opening:
  100 * pieces + 30 * threats + 3 * mobility

Midgame/endgame (after the terminal checks):
  1000 * pieces + 50 * threats + 20 * blocked(Black - White)
  + 10 * mills + 5 * mobility

then limited to the range -9999 .. 9999.

The weights are ordered so that one piece outweighs any realistic
combination of the positional features (for example, 1000 per piece versus
at most a few threats at 50 each in the midgame). The positional features
therefore decide between moves that come out even in material, which is
exactly where the handout estimate has nothing to go on.


4. Why it is better than the handout estimate
---------------------------------------------

- It still agrees with the handout on material: when one move wins a piece
  and another does not, both estimates prefer the capture.
- When material comes out the same at the search horizon, the handout
  estimate falls back to move order (first move found). The improved
  estimate instead prefers moves that create mill threats, block the
  opponent's threats, and keep pieces mobile.
- It recognizes a blocked White as a loss.
- It looks one step past the search horizon: a threat at a leaf position
  is a capture that the search would only see one ply deeper.


5. Example positions (depth 3)
------------------------------

Each pair was produced by running the original and improved programs on
the same input file. The search, move order, and tie-break are identical,
so the different move comes only from the estimate.

Board locations: a0 d0 g0 b1 d1 f1 c2 e2 a3 b3 c3 e3 f3 g3 c4 d4 e4 b5 d5 f5
a6 d6 g6 = indices 0..22.


Example 1 - Opening: creating more mill threats
  Input:     xxxxWxxWxxBWBxxBxxWxxxB

  python MiniMaxOpening.py           Board Position: xxWxWxxWxxBWBxxBxxWxxxB
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 2
                                     (White places at g0)

  python MiniMaxOpeningImproved.py   Board Position: xxxxWWxWxxBWBxxBxxWxxxB
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 257
                                     (White places at f1)

  Why f1: it creates three mill threats at once: b1 (b1-d1-f1), g0
  (g0-f1-e2), and e4 (e2-e3-e4). g0 creates only two (f1 and e4). Black can
  block only one per move, so f1 leaves White more ways to close a mill
  even after Black blocks or builds its own threat. The handout estimate
  sees only the piece count, which is the same after every placement here,
  so it picks g0 because it comes first in board order.


Example 2 - Opening: which piece to capture
  Input:     xxxxxxxWWWxBxxxBxxBxxxx

  python MiniMaxOpening.py           Board Position: xxxxxxxWWWWBxxxxxxBxxxx
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 2
                                     (White places at c3, closes a3-b3-c3,
                                      removes Black's d4)

  python MiniMaxOpeningImproved.py   Board Position: xxxxxxxWWWWBxxxBxxxxxxx
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 212
                                     (White places at c3, closes a3-b3-c3,
                                      removes Black's d5)

  Why d5: Black had d4 and d5 in a line with d6 empty (a Black mill
  threat). Removing either piece stops it, so the handout estimate sees
  both captures as equal and takes d4 by board order. d5 is a junction
  point with four neighbors (d4, b5, f5, d6), while d4 has three, so
  removing d5 takes away more of Black's future mobility and leaves
  Black's remaining pieces further apart.


Example 3 - Midgame: blocking the opponent's mill threat
  Input:     WxxBBxWWxBWBBWWxWWBxxxx

  python MiniMaxGame.py              Board Position: WxxBBxWWxBWBBWxWWWBxxxx
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 1993
                                     (White moves c4 -> d4)

  python MiniMaxGameImproved.py      Board Position: WxxBBWWxxBWBBWWxWWBxxxx
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 2070
                                     (White moves e2 -> f1)

  Why e2 -> f1: Black has b1 and d1 with f1 empty, and Black's f3 can move
  into f1 to close b1-d1-f1. The handout's choice ignores this: its best
  line lets Black play f3 -> f1 and capture White's c2, and White wins the
  piece back afterward. The improved move occupies f1, removing Black's
  only mill threat, and keeps White's c2-c3-c4 mill intact. White is
  already two pieces ahead, so preventing Black's capture keeps that
  advantage without relying on a trade.


Example 4 - Midgame: which piece to capture after closing a mill
  Input:     xxWxxBBWxBBBWxWWxWWWWBB

  python MiniMaxGame.py              Board Position: xxWxxxBWxBBBWxWWWWWxWBB
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 3995
                                     (White moves f5 -> e4, closes
                                      c4-d4-e4, removes Black's f1)

  python MiniMaxGameImproved.py      Board Position: xxWxxBBWxBBBWxWWWWWxWxB
                                     MINIMAX estimate: 4105
                                     (White moves f5 -> e4, closes
                                      c4-d4-e4, removes Black's d6)

  Why d6: removing d6 opens the d4-d5-d6 line, and White's a6 is next to
  d6, so White immediately has a new mill threat there. Black has to spend
  its next move blocking d6 (with g6), while White keeps the e4/f5 piece
  that can move back and forth between c4-d4-e4 and b5-d5-f5, closing a
  mill on each move. The handout estimate counts the same number of pieces
  after every capture choice, so it removes f1, the first Black piece in
  board order.


6. Limitations
--------------

- Weights were chosen by reasoning about the game, not tuned by playing
  many games.
- Threats are counted per empty spot. A threat the opponent can block on
  their very next move counts the same as one they cannot block.
- The estimate does not know whose turn it is at the leaf position.
